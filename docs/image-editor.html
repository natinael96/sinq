Sinq · design note

An image editor that fits the text.

What a share-card editor for ስንቅ has to do, what it must not do, and what the screen looks like. Researched against the renderer that ships today.

What ships today

PassageShare.kt is a good renderer and not an editor. It draws a 1080 px card in the green-and-gold identity, with three frames and two grounds. Every type size in it is a constant: kicker 34, title 58, body 44, colophon 34, line spacing 1.5. The reader chooses a shape and a ground and nothing else.

It is also losing text, silently

The paginator splits a long passage into pages sized against MAX_H (1920 px) no matter which frame was chosen. The renderer then lays that page into the frame the reader actually picked and ellipsizes whatever will not fit. The two never agree except on a story.

Nothing warns anyone. A reader shares a square of a long ምስባክ and posts a passage with most of it replaced by an ellipsis. This is the case for the editor in one sentence: the type size has to answer to the text, not the other way round.

Research: what this app's editor actually needs

1. Ethiopic sets the typography, and it is not Latin

Abyssinica SIL has tall ascenders and deep descenders, so a line spacing that looks generous in Latin looks cramped in ግዕዝ. The site already sets Ethiopic at 1.85; the renderer uses 1.5 and it is tight. More consequentially, Ethiopic has no letter case. Every hierarchy trick that leans on caps — small caps, uppercase eyebrows, all-caps labels — is simply unavailable. Hierarchy has to come from size, weight, colour and space alone, which is why the editor should expose those four and nothing decorative.

Two more that are specific to this corpus: verse numbers belong in Ge'ez numerals (፩ ፪ ፫), not Arabic; and the printed books set the salutation opening each መልክእ stanza and the Name of God in red. The app already honours that in the reader. A card that flattens it to one colour is losing something the books consider part of the text.

2. Auto-size means fit, not shrink

The naive reading of "decide the text size" is one number that shrinks until the text fits. That produces a 14 px wall of type for a long reading and a lonely line adrift in a story frame for a short one. What the card actually wants is a size chosen from the length of the passage, clamped at both ends, with overflow going to a second page rather than to a smaller font:

The floor is the important half. Below roughly 40 px at 1080 the Ethiopic stops being comfortable at the size a phone actually shows a shared image, and a card nobody can read is worse than a card that says "1 of 3".

3. The frames that matter here are not the generic set

This app's passages travel through Telegram and WhatsApp Status in Ethiopian and diaspora circles — that is how the ማኅሌት corpus in the app reached it in the first place. Story is not an afterthought; for this audience it may be the main one, and it is the only frame with platform chrome to design around.

4. Presets, not a design tool

The person reaching for this is sharing a verse, not designing. A full editor — every size, colour and offset on a slider — is the wrong instrument and would take longer than the sharing it serves. The shape that works is four or five named presets that set everything at once, with a short drawer of the few adjustments worth having. Most people will never open the drawer, and that is the design working.

The architecture

One immutable CardSpec, one pure renderer, two consumers. The editor mutates the spec; the preview renders it at 540 px for speed; export renders the identical function at 1080 or 2160. Nothing about the card can drift between what the reader saw and what they sent, because there is only one function that knows how to draw it.

CardSpec

frame, ground, preset, body size, line spacing, alignment, block position, margins, what the colophon shows. Serialisable, so the last card can be remembered.

fit()

Chooses the body size from the passage length, then measures with StaticLayout and paginates whatever is still over. Pure, and testable without a device.

render(spec, scale)

The existing PassageShare drawing, with every constant read from the spec instead of the file. One code path for preview and export.

Editor screen

Holds a spec in state, redraws the preview on change, and hands the final spec to share or save — the two functions that already exist.

The first commit should be the fit, not the editor. Replacing the ellipsis with a real fit-and-paginate fixes text loss for everyone immediately, including readers who never open an editor. The editor is then a way to override a decision that is already correct by default.

Why this shape

The preview never moves. It stays the same size on the stage whichever frame is chosen, so switching from square to story is a change in the card and not a jump in the layout. Controls that move their own target under the finger are the usual failure of editors like this.

Frames sit between the card and the sheet, because the frame is the one decision that changes everything below it. Choosing it first is the natural order, and putting it on the card's own edge says so without a label.

Five tabs, and the first one is usually the last one. Style carries the presets and the two adjustments most likely to be wanted — size and air. Ground, Type, Layout and Mark exist for the person who wants them and cost nothing to the person who does not.

Size reads "Auto · 68" rather than a bare number. It says the app already chose, and shows what it chose. Dragging the track takes it off auto; a tap on the word puts it back. That is the whole of the relationship between the automatic decision and the manual one.

Ge'ez sits under Style, not Type. Switching ግእዝ/አማርኛ changes the words, not the typography — and it changes the length, which is what the size is chosen from. It belongs beside the thing it moves.

What I would not build

Free text placement

Dragging the block anywhere turns every card into a different card. Three positions keep the family recognisable, which is the point of a template.

Arbitrary colours

The green and the gold are the app's identity and the reason a forwarded card is recognisable. A colour wheel dissolves that in an afternoon.

Stickers and frames

Wrong register for the material. The text is ቅዱስ; the decoration should stay at the edge and stay quiet.

Editing the passage

The words come from the bundled text and should stay exactly as the sources set them. An editable field is a way to put words in the Church's mouth.
